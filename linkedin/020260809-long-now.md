# Why the Year is 02026 (The Case for 10,000-Year Thinking)

**Date:** 02026-08-09  
**Original Post:** [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/creativemaladjustment_why-the-year-is-02026-the-case-for-10000-activity-7492256701539258368-Jn-u)

If you have seen me date a document as 02026, you might think it is a typo. It is actually a deliberate choice to shift my perspective from three-month goals to a 10,000-year view of humanity. 

In the late 90s, I came across @The Long Now Foundation and noticed our collective thinking was shrinking. As we approached the year 2000, we stopped looking past it. We moved from long-term vision to three-year plans with three-month goals. I believe that when you look way out, your priorities and profit margins change for the better. You begin to think massively, but act now.

I learned the importance of this first-hand at a previous company that managed digital media at remote locations. We had a system where users could "park" content by setting end dates far into the future. One day, someone entered the year "9999." Although our central system allowed the data entry, the software at the remote sites could not process a date that exceeded the Microsoft 64-bit data type limit. The entire network crashed and the displays went dark.

If we had been "YfiveK compliant," we might have seen that failure coming. I use five-digit years (YYYYY) as a daily reminder to build systems and relationships that are resilient enough to last beyond our immediate horizon.

I am curious about the legacy of your work. When you stop looking at the next quarter and start looking at the next century, what changes in your approach? I would love to hear how you balance today's "paper work" with the "poetry" of a 10,000-year future.
